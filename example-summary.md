### Paper Summary (50–80 words)
This paper reports a single case of a urethral diverticulum, an uncommon complication, occurring after a tubularised incised plate repair for distal hypospadias in a 2-year-old child. The objective is to present the case details, diagnostic process, and surgical management. The approach is a descriptive case report detailing the patient's presentation, the surgical intervention (partial excision, urethroplasty, and meatoplasty), and the three-month follow-up outcome.

### Clinical Relevance (100–130 words)
The authors state the objective is to present a case of postoperative urethral diverticulum after distal hypospadias repair and discuss treatment options. They position this as important for raising awareness because, although rare (cited as 0.3% of complications), it causes significant voiding dysfunction. The proposed clinical application is for physicians to educate parents about the signs and symptoms (e.g., ballooning, dribbling) and the necessity of regular follow-up for early management. The authors explicitly compare their case to the literature, noting diverticula are mainly associated with proximal hypospadias, making this distal case noteworthy. They also compare practices, strongly stating that circumcision is contraindicated in hypospadias and that repair is technically easier in uncircumcised children.

### Methodology (200–250 words)
The study design is a single case report. The explicit objective is to report a case of urethral diverticulum formation after distal hypospadias repair and discuss possible treatment options. The data source is one patient: a male child who presented in 2019. The sample characteristics include a history of distal penile hypospadias, a missed diagnosis at birth leading to a contraindicated Plastibell circumcision, and a subsequent tubularised incised plate urethroplasty at 18 months of age. The "intervention" described is the diagnostic and therapeutic management for the diverticulum. Diagnosis involved clinical evaluation, ultrasound (showing post-void residual volume), ascending urethrogram (confirming meatal stenosis and diverticulum), and cystourethroscopy (confirming diverticulum extent). The treatment was a surgical procedure consisting of partial excision of the diverticulum, urethroplasty (closed in two layers and supported by Dartos fascia), and standard meatoplasty for meatal stenosis. The experimental setup is the clinical follow-up. The sole comparison condition or baseline is the patient's pre-operative symptomatic state. The evaluation metrics are clinical outcomes: resolution of symptoms (thin stream, ballooning, post-void dribbling) and observation of a "normal thick continuous stream" at the 3-month follow-up.

### Results (200–250 words)
The reported descriptive outcome is the complete resolution of the patient's symptoms three months after surgical repair. Pre-operatively, the child presented with a thin forceful stream, ballooning of the ventral penis during voiding, significant post-void dribbling, and need for manual emptying. Diagnostic results showed an increased post-void residual volume on ultrasound, meatal stenosis with a proximal diverticulum on ascending urethrogram, and a diverticulum extending from the subcorona to the base of the penis on cystourethroscopy. Post-operatively, at the 3-month follow-up, the child had a normal thick continuous stream with no post-void dribbling or urethral ballooning. The results directly address the aim of discussing treatment options by demonstrating that the performed surgical procedure (partial excision, urethroplasty, and meatoplasty) was successful in this specific case, leading to symptomatic resolution.
